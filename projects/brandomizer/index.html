01

Why I made it

Colour palette generators are pure luck. You keep pressing until something looks right.

I sometimes use a website that generates colour palettes. It's useful, but it's just luck: it throws out random colours and you keep going until you see something you like. There's no idea behind it.

So I thought it'd be cool if a tool could generate palettes from a theme instead, so "Seventies" gives you something that actually feels seventies. Then I took it a step further and had it pick the fonts that go with the theme too, a headline and a body pairing, so you get the start of a whole brand.

And if it's luck either way, it may as well look like it. For the container I wanted something funky, so it became an old-style fruit machine. It started as a fun idea, and it's ended up one of my favourite things I've made.

02

How to play

1Pick a theme

Tap a row on the pay table, or press THEME. Each theme sets its own colour rules and font list.

2Pull the handle

The reels spin as real drums and land one by one. Your brand name is spelled out in the headline font.

3Hold and re-spin

Hold the colours or fonts you love, and they stay put while the rest spin again.

4Cash out

A brand card prints into the tray, with every value, the fonts and a contrast check.

03

From flat to fruit machine

The first version was a flat 2D page: a machine-shaped panel with colour swatches, letter reels and a lever on the side. It proved the idea worked, but it didn't feel like pulling a real handle. So I rebuilt it as a full 3D machine in three.js, with drums that physically spin, chrome, bulbs and a tray that prints a card.

Little nods to me

Inspected by H.G., established 2001

Like a real machine, it carries a maker's plate and an inspection sticker. The green "Inspected" stamp is signed H.G., my initials. The plate says "Est. 2001", the year I was born, and the serial number, 0002001, hides it again.

The warning sticker

Fruit machines tell you to gamble responsibly. This one tells you to design responsibly, with a few warnings every designer will recognise.

INSERT
IDEAANY SIZE

Insert idea, any size

The coin slot doesn't take money. It says "Insert idea, any size", and dropping an idea in starts a spin. Underneath it still says 10p, 20p, 50p.

A pay table of themes

Where a fruit machine lists its prizes, this one lists the 16 themes, each with a palette preview and a named "house pick", like Avocado Suite for the seventies or Dial-Up Dreams for Y2K.

1 IN 40JACKPOT

A real jackpot

About one spin in forty hits the jackpot: it lands on the theme's hand-picked house pick, with flashing bulbs and a bell.

Every brand card is a payout

Cashing out prints a numbered card into the tray, "Payout No." and all. Click it for the hex, RGB and HSL values, the font links, CSS variables to copy and a PNG or JPEG to save.

AAA 3CONTRAST, ALWAYS CHECKED

Always usable

Every palette has at least one dark and one light colour, so there's always a readable text pair, and the machine shows a live accessibility contrast count for every spin.

Sounds made from scratch

The reels, bell and coin are all synthesised in the browser rather than recorded. And for anyone who'd rather not use a mouse, the whole machine works from the keyboard.

05

How it's built

It's one HTML page with no build step. The machine is modelled in code with three.js, its artwork is drawn on canvases, and bloom makes the bulbs glow. The camera opens at an angle and settles on the machine, and set views jump between the machine, the reels and the tray.
